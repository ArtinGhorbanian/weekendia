# Roadmap

Weekendia's roadmap describes direction for private evaluation, not commitments, dates, or guaranteed scope. Rights clearance and safety review take precedence over feature work.

## Current: establish a trustworthy private baseline

- Complete repeatable unit, static, browser-flow, responsive, and dependency checks.
- Validate installation and demo operation from a clean clone.
- Keep configuration examples, security boundaries, decision records, and user-facing limitations aligned with the implementation.
- Resolve source origin, contributor authority, visual-asset provenance, naming clearance, and project-license questions.
- Keep the repository private until every publication gate has documented evidence.

## Next: deepen product evaluation

- Expand deterministic test cases for prompt boundaries, formatter safety, browser error recovery, and accessibility behavior.
- Add an editable review step between generated recommendations and saved itinerary text.
- Explore structured provider output while preserving readable fallback errors and strict rendering rules.
- Improve venue-verification prompts without implying access to live data.
- Define an approved data-retention policy and document model-provider configuration controls.

## Later: design an approved deployment path

- Specify authentication, authorization, session handling, and abuse controls.
- Replace process-local request limits with shared controls appropriate to the chosen topology.
- Establish production server, TLS, monitoring, backup, incident response, and secret-management requirements.
- Evaluate optional provider adapters only after privacy, reliability, cost, and licensing review.
- Conduct a documented threat-model and accessibility review against the intended hosting environment.

## Publication gate

Public release, redistribution, or commercialization remains out of scope until authorized reviewers document:

1. source, contributor, event, employer, client, and third-party asset provenance;
2. project-name and visual-identity clearance;
3. a deliberate project-license decision and any required notices;
4. secret-history review and credential rotation where needed;
5. privacy, security, accessibility, deployment, and support ownership appropriate to the intended audience.

## Non-goals for the current build

- Live booking, payment, reservation, pricing, transport, or weather services.
- Emergency guidance or guarantees that a suggestion is safe, accessible, available, or within budget.
- Public multi-user hosting without a separately approved operational design.
- Claims of ownership, originality, external provenance, performance outcomes, or legal clearance without evidence.
