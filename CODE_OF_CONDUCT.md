# Code of Conduct

Weekendia's private collaboration space should be safe, focused, and respectful. This standard applies to repository discussions, reviews, issues, private project channels, demonstrations, and other work conducted on the project's behalf.

## Expected conduct

- Engage with ideas and evidence without attacking the person presenting them.
- Give specific, constructive feedback and make room for questions and differing levels of experience.
- Respect boundaries, privacy, access controls, and requests to stop an unwelcome interaction.
- Use inclusive language and consider accessibility in both communication and product decisions.
- Disclose relevant conflicts, uncertainty, provenance concerns, and mistakes promptly.
- Protect credentials, personal information, private repository content, and vulnerability details.

## Unacceptable conduct

- Harassment, intimidation, discrimination, threats, stalking, or unwanted sexual attention.
- Personal insults, deliberate humiliation, sustained disruption, or retaliation for a good-faith report.
- Publishing private communications or identifying information without authorization.
- Pressuring someone to bypass security, rights, review, or disclosure controls.
- Knowingly misrepresenting authorship, test results, product capabilities, vulnerabilities, or project status.

## Raising a concern

Report conduct concerns through the existing private maintainer or project-moderation channel. Include only the information needed to understand the incident, preserve relevant evidence, and avoid broad redistribution. If the usual recipient is involved, use another authorized project representative.

Reports should be handled discreetly and shared only with people needed to assess and respond. Absolute confidentiality cannot be promised, but unnecessary disclosure and retaliation are not acceptable.

Security vulnerabilities follow the separate private process in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).

## Response

Authorized project moderators may seek clarification, set communication boundaries, remove content, restrict participation, or revoke repository access. The response should consider severity, impact, context, prior behavior, and the safety of affected people. A person subject to action may request a review through a different authorized project representative when one is available.

This policy defines collaboration expectations; it does not determine employment, ownership, authorship, or licensing rights.
